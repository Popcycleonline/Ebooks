Communication & Couples Work
Worth Understanding First

Most relationship problems are communication problems in disguise.

Gottman's research found that the way couples communicate about small, everyday things predicts how they handle the big ones. The products in this section work because they address communication patterns at the root rather than offering advice for individual conflicts. The goal is not to win arguments better. It is to need fewer of them.

01 of 08
The Gottman Institute
Evidence-Based Tools for Stronger Relationships
Dr. John Gottman spent over 40 years studying couples in a research lab, observing their interactions and following up years later to see who stayed together and who did not. The patterns he identified are remarkably consistent: the couples who thrive are not the ones who fight less, but the ones who fight differently. They maintain what Gottman calls a "positive sentiment override," stay curious rather than defensive, and repair quickly after conflict. The Gottman Institute's online courses translate these findings into practical daily exercises any couple can use, no therapist required, though therapist-guided options are available too.

Best For: Any couple wanting real tools

Honest Answers

Questions we actually get

Q. Should we try a couples app or couples therapy first?
If things feel manageable but could be better, start with an app like Lasting. The daily practice of a well-designed app can address a lot before it becomes a crisis, and it is significantly more affordable than therapy sessions. If you are dealing with a specific recurring issue that has not improved on its own, or if one or both partners feel stuck, couples therapy with a specialist like those on Ours is the more appropriate starting point.
Q. Does online couples therapy actually work?
Research comparing in-person and online couples therapy shows similar outcomes for most presenting concerns. The biggest advantage of online therapy is that it removes the logistics barrier, which means couples are more likely to actually attend consistently. Consistency matters more than the medium. That said, for complex situations involving trauma, severe mental health concerns, or high-conflict dynamics, in-person work with a specialist may be preferable.
Q. Is Match or eHarmony better for people over 50?
Both have strong user bases in the over-50 demographic, but they work differently. Match gives you more browsing control and a larger pool of potential matches. eHarmony does more of the filtering for you based on compatibility dimensions and tends to produce fewer but better-matched suggestions. If you prefer to browse and decide, Match. If you want the algorithm to do the heavy lifting, eHarmony.
Q. What if only one partner wants to work on the relationship?
This is more common than most people realize, and individual therapy is often the right starting point. Working on your own communication patterns, attachment style, and responses can meaningfully shift the dynamic in a relationship even if only one person is actively in therapy. The Gottman Institute also has resources specifically for this situation. Change in one person almost always produces some change in the system around them.
