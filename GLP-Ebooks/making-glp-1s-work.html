Start Here

The prescription is the start,
not the finish

A GLP-1 gives you something powerful: a quiet appetite and a real chance to lose significant weight. But what you do with that opening determines everything about the quality and durability of your results. The medication opens the door; you still have to walk through it well.

Here's the core issue this guide exists to solve. When you lose weight — on any method, but especially rapidly — some of that loss comes from muscle, not just fat. On a GLP-1, studies suggest a substantial share of total weight lost can be lean mass, sometimes a quarter to 40%. Losing muscle is the opposite of what you want: it weakens you, slows your metabolism (making regain easier), and undermines the health you're trying to build. And the drug does nothing to prevent it — that protection is entirely in your hands.

The good news is that the solution is well established and completely within reach: adequate protein and resistance training preserve muscle while you lose fat. The challenge is that the medication's appetite suppression makes eating enough protein genuinely harder, and low energy can make training feel daunting. This playbook is built around solving exactly those practical problems — so your weight loss is the good kind, and so it lasts.

"Anyone can lose weight on a GLP-1. The goal is to lose the right weight — fat, not muscle — and to build the habits that hold when the easy part is over. That's the whole game."

The Core Principle

Your job on a GLP-1 is to make sure the weight you lose is fat.

The medication handles appetite. Your job is everything that steers the loss toward fat and away from muscle — and that comes down to two things above all: eating enough protein and doing resistance training. Everything else in this guide (managing side effects, staying hydrated, sleeping well) exists to make those two possible. Get the protein and the lifting right, and you transform "getting smaller" into "getting leaner, stronger, and healthier" — a fundamentally better outcome.

Part OneProtect Your Muscle
The Two Non-Negotiables

How to lose fat
and keep your strength

If you do nothing else in this guide, do these two things. Together, protein and resistance training are what determine whether your weight loss builds a healthier body or a weaker one.

01Why
Understand the Risk

Why Muscle Loss Happens — and Why It Matters

When you're in a calorie deficit, your body draws energy from both fat and muscle. Rapid loss and low protein intake tilt that balance toward more muscle — and GLP-1s create exactly those conditions by sharply reducing how much you eat. Losing muscle matters enormously: it weakens you, impairs balance and function (especially as you age), and lowers your resting metabolism, which makes weight regain easier. Preserving muscle isn't vanity — it's what keeps your metabolism healthy and your body capable.

The StakesMuscle is metabolically active and functionally essential. Losing it makes you weaker and slows your metabolism — undermining both your health and your ability to keep the weight off. This is the risk the rest of this section prevents.
02Fix One
Prioritize Protein

Hit Your Protein Target, Every Day

Protein is the raw material your body uses to preserve and build muscle, and its needs rise during weight loss. Clinical guidance for people on GLP-1s generally lands around 1.2–2.0 grams of protein per kilogram of body weight per day, spread across meals rather than crammed into one. The challenge is real: with appetite suppressed, hitting that target takes intention. But protein is the single most important nutritional lever you have for protecting muscle — so it comes first, before other foods, at every meal.

The TargetRoughly 1.2–2.0 g/kg of body weight daily, distributed across your meals. Because appetite is reduced, this usually requires deliberate planning and often a protein supplement to bridge the gap. Your provider or a dietitian can help set your specific number.
03Fix Two
Lift Something

Resistance Training Is Non-Negotiable

Protein provides the building blocks, but resistance training is the signal that tells your body to keep the muscle. The research is consistent: pairing a GLP-1 with resistance training preserves muscle and maintains weight loss better than the medication alone. You don't need to become a bodybuilder — two to three sessions a week of basic strength work (bodyweight, bands, or weights) targeting all the major muscle groups is enough to dramatically change the quality of your weight loss. This is the exercise that matters most here; cardio is a bonus.

The Minimum Effective Dose2–3 resistance sessions weekly, hitting the major muscle groups with compound movements. If you're new to it, our Beginner's Strength Training Over 40 guide walks you through exactly how to start safely.
04Note
A Word for Older Adults

The Stakes Are Higher After 60

Muscle preservation matters at every age, but it becomes especially critical for older adults, who already face age-related muscle loss and for whom weakness and falls carry serious consequences. If you're older, the protein-and-resistance-training combination isn't optional — it's essential to ensure the medication improves your health rather than accelerating frailty. One caution: very high protein intake can stress the kidneys in people with kidney issues, and protein supplements aren't right for everyone, so older adults especially should set their protein plan with a doctor.

The PriorityFor older adults, protecting muscle and strength is the difference between weight loss that extends healthy independence and weight loss that undermines it. Work with your provider to get the protein target and training plan right for you.
The Whole Point

"The scale can't tell the difference between losing fat and losing muscle. Your body can — and so will your metabolism, your strength, and your future self. Protect the muscle."

Part TwoThe Daily Playbook
Living It Day to Day

Six habits that make
the whole thing work

These are the practical, everyday moves that make protein achievable, keep side effects manageable, and help you feel good enough to actually train. This is the daily texture of doing GLP-1s well.

1
Eat Protein First, at Every Meal

With limited appetite, the order you eat in matters. Eat your protein first — before the vegetables, starches, or anything else — so that if you fill up quickly (and you will), you've already gotten the most important nutrient in. This simple sequencing habit, sometimes called "protein first," is one of the highest-impact changes you can make on a GLP-1. It ensures the small amount you do eat is working hardest to protect your muscle, rather than filling you up with less essential foods.

The HabitAt every meal, take several bites of the protein before touching anything else. When fullness hits early, you'll have banked the nutrient that matters most for muscle. Simple, free, and remarkably effective.
2
Use Protein Supplements to Bridge the Gap

For most people on a GLP-1, hitting a protein target through whole food alone becomes genuinely difficult once appetite drops — solid food fills you up fast. This is exactly what protein supplements are for. A protein shake or a scoop of quality powder delivers 20–30 grams in a form that's easy to consume even when you're not hungry, making the difference between hitting your target and falling short. This isn't a gimmick; it's a practical tool for a real problem the medication creates.

The ToolA third-party-tested whey or plant protein you actually like the taste of. A shake can be far easier to get down than a chicken breast when appetite is low. (If you have kidney concerns, clear supplement use with your doctor first.)
3
Manage Nausea Proactively

Nausea is the most common early side effect, and a few habits reduce it substantially: eat smaller portions, eat slowly, and stop at the first sign of fullness rather than pushing to finish. Avoid greasy, very rich, or heavy meals, which tend to sit poorly. Ginger (tea, chews) helps some people. Nausea usually eases as your body adjusts and is worst after a dose increase, so patience through the adjustment period helps. If nausea is severe or persistent, tell your prescriber — dose adjustments and anti-nausea options exist.

The ApproachSmall, slow, and stop-when-full. Skip the heavy, greasy meals. Lean on ginger. Expect the roughest patch right after dose increases, and know it typically settles. Persistent severe nausea is a conversation with your doctor, not something to endure silently.
4
Stay Ahead of Constipation With Fiber and Fluids

Because you're eating less and digestion slows, constipation is common. Getting enough fiber (from vegetables, fruit, whole grains, or a soluble-fiber supplement) and drinking plenty of water addresses it directly — and fiber has a bonus, since it genuinely supports your body's own satiety signals. Gentle daily movement helps too. This is a small, manageable issue when you stay ahead of it, and an uncomfortable one when you don't, so build the fiber-and-water habit early rather than reacting later.

The HabitPrioritize fiber-rich foods, consider a simple soluble-fiber supplement, and drink water consistently through the day. Staying ahead of constipation is far easier than fixing it after the fact.
5
Hydrate and Watch Electrolytes

When you eat and drink less, dehydration and low electrolytes can sneak up on you — showing up as fatigue, headaches, dizziness, or muscle cramps that can easily be mistaken for the medication "not agreeing" with you. Deliberately drinking water throughout the day, and getting adequate electrolytes (sodium, potassium, magnesium) through food or a sensible electrolyte supplement, keeps you feeling far better and makes it possible to train. Low energy is often just dehydration in disguise.

The FixKeep water visible and sip through the day rather than relying on thirst (which is blunted when you eat less). If you feel persistently drained, electrolytes are worth addressing before assuming it's the drug itself.
6
Don't Under-Eat — Fuel the Muscle and the Training

It sounds counterintuitive on a weight-loss medication, but eating too little is a real risk on a GLP-1, because appetite suppression can drive intake so low that you can't get enough protein or energy to preserve muscle and support training. The goal isn't to eat as little as possible — it's to lose fat while staying nourished and strong. If you're routinely eating tiny amounts and feeling weak, that's a signal to prioritize protein-dense foods and, if needed, talk to your provider or a dietitian. Adequate, protein-forward nourishment is part of the plan, not a violation of it.

The ReframeUnder-eating isn't a virtue here — it's a threat to your muscle and your energy. Aim for enough protein-rich nourishment to train and preserve strength, not for the lowest possible number. If you feel weak or unwell, seek professional guidance.

Real Questions

What people actually
want to know

QExactly how much protein should I be eating?
General clinical guidance for people on GLP-1s lands around 1.2–2.0 grams of protein per kilogram of body weight per day, spread across meals — meaningfully more than the baseline recommendation for sedentary adults, because you're actively trying to preserve muscle during weight loss. That said, the right number for you depends on your body, activity level, and health, including kidney function, so it's worth setting a specific target with your doctor or a registered dietitian rather than guessing. The practical takeaway: aim high on protein, prioritize it at every meal, and use a supplement if whole food falls short.
QDo I really need to exercise, or can I just take the medication?
You can lose weight without exercising, but you'll likely lose more muscle along with the fat — which undermines your metabolism, strength, and long-term results. Resistance training is what signals your body to hold onto muscle while you lose fat, and research consistently shows the medication-plus-training combination produces better body composition and more durable results than the drug alone. You don't need much — two or three short strength sessions a week is enough to make a real difference. If you skip one thing, don't let it be this. It's the highest-value habit for the quality of your outcome.
QI'm too exhausted to work out. What do I do?
First, check the basics, because low energy on a GLP-1 is often fixable: are you getting enough protein, enough total food, enough water and electrolytes, and enough sleep? Under-eating and dehydration are common, sneaky causes of fatigue here. Address those first. Then start smaller than you think — even brief, gentle strength sessions preserve muscle, and something is vastly better than nothing. If profound fatigue persists despite covering the basics, tell your provider, since it can occasionally signal an issue that needs attention. Often, though, fixing nutrition and hydration restores enough energy to train.
QWhat if the nausea or side effects are really affecting me?
Don't just tough it out in silence — talk to your prescriber. Side effects that significantly affect your quality of life or your ability to eat adequately are a medical conversation, and there are real solutions: slowing down your dose escalation, staying at a lower dose longer, adjusting timing, or adding supportive treatments. The habits in this guide (small slow meals, avoiding heavy foods, ginger, hydration) help with typical adjustment-period symptoms, but persistent or severe side effects deserve professional attention rather than endurance. The goal is to find a sustainable approach, not to suffer through an intolerable one.
QHow do I keep the weight off if I stop the medication?
This is where the habits you build now become everything. Weight regain after stopping is common precisely because appetite returns — but the people who fare best are those who used their time on the medication to build genuine, durable habits: a protein-forward way of eating, a consistent resistance-training routine, and the muscle mass those preserve. That muscle keeps your metabolism higher, and the habits keep your intake in check even without the appetite suppression. There are no guarantees, and many people stay on a maintenance dose long-term, which is legitimate. But whatever you decide, the strength and habits you build now are the best insurance you have.
