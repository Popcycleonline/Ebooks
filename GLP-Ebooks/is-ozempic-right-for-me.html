What You Actually Need to Know

The GLP-1 revolution —
what it is and why it matters

In 2021, the FDA approved semaglutide (Wegovy) for chronic weight management. The clinical trial results were unlike anything seen before in obesity medicine — participants lost an average of 15-17% of body weight over 68 weeks.

GLP-1 receptor agonists work by mimicking a hormone your body naturally produces after eating. They slow gastric emptying, signal fullness to the brain, reduce appetite, and appear to change how the brain relates to food at a neurological level. People on these medications often report that food simply stops being as compelling. The constant background noise of food thoughts quiets down. For many people who have struggled with weight their entire lives, this is a categorically new experience.

None of this makes them right for everyone. Side effects are real. The cost is significant. What happens when you stop matters enormously. This guide covers all of it.

"GLP-1 medications don't work despite your lifestyle. They work best because of it."

The Mechanism

How GLP-1 medications actually work in your body

GLP-1 is a hormone secreted by your intestines in response to food. GLP-1 receptor agonists bind to these same receptors and activate them continuously — producing sustained appetite suppression, reduced food cravings, and slowed digestion. Tirzepatide also activates GIP receptors, which appears to enhance the effect further.

  • Slows gastric emptying — food stays in your stomach longer, fullness lasts longer
  • Reduces appetite signals in the brain — hunger becomes less urgent
  • Decreases food cravings — many users report reduced interest in high-calorie foods specifically
  • Improves insulin sensitivity — beneficial for people with or at risk of Type 2 diabetes
  • May have cardiovascular benefits — SELECT trial showed 20% reduction in major cardiac events
Part OneThe Medications — What's Available
Know Your Options

The main GLP-1 medications
compared

The GLP-1 class has expanded rapidly. Understanding the differences matters for your conversation with your doctor.

01Drug
FDA Approved 2021

Semaglutide — Ozempic / Wegovy

The most studied GLP-1 medication for weight loss. Ozempic (lower dose) is FDA-approved for Type 2 diabetes. Wegovy (higher dose) is FDA-approved specifically for chronic weight management in adults with BMI ≥30, or ≥27 with a weight-related condition. The STEP trials showed average weight loss of 14.9-17.4% over 68 weeks. Administered as a weekly subcutaneous injection, with doses titrated up over 16-20 weeks.

Key PointWegovy is the weight-loss-approved form. Ozempic prescribed off-label for weight loss is clinically identical but may face insurance coverage challenges.
02Drug
FDA Approved 2023

Tirzepatide — Mounjaro / Zepbound

A dual GIP/GLP-1 receptor agonist — it activates two pathways simultaneously. Mounjaro is approved for Type 2 diabetes; Zepbound is approved for weight management. The SURMOUNT trials showed average weight loss of 20.9-22.5% at the highest dose — the largest weight loss seen in any non-surgical clinical trial. Also a weekly injection. Many endocrinologists now consider it the most effective option available.

Key PointIf semaglutide produces good but not great results, tirzepatide is often the next clinical step. Eli Lilly manufactures both.
03Drug
FDA Approved 2010

Liraglutide — Saxenda

The first GLP-1 approved for weight management, requiring daily injection rather than weekly. Average weight loss in trials was 5-8% — meaningful but lower than newer options. Less commonly prescribed as first-line now that weekly options with better efficacy exist, but may be appropriate as an alternative or for cost reasons.

Key PointWorth knowing about as a lower-cost or alternative option, but the weekly injectables have largely superseded it for new prescriptions.
04Drug
Oral Option

Oral Semaglutide — Rybelsus

An oral form of semaglutide, currently FDA-approved only for Type 2 diabetes. Produces less weight loss than injectable semaglutide due to lower oral bioavailability. Some physicians prescribe off-label for weight management, particularly for patients who prefer not to inject. An oral weight-loss-specific approval at higher doses has been submitted to the FDA.

Key PointIf you have needle aversion, oral GLP-1 options are coming — worth discussing with your doctor as the category evolves.
Part TwoAre You a Candidate?
Eligibility & Considerations

Who these medications are —
and aren't — for

FDA approval criteria are the starting point, not the full picture. Your physician will assess your complete health profile before prescribing.

1
FDA Eligibility Criteria

For weight management (Wegovy/Zepbound): adults with BMI ≥30, OR adults with BMI ≥27 who also have at least one weight-related condition such as Type 2 diabetes, high blood pressure, high cholesterol, or sleep apnea. Off-label prescribing exists outside these parameters.

Important NoteBMI is an imperfect measure. Some physicians consider metabolic health markers and body composition alongside BMI when making prescribing decisions.
2
Who Should Not Take These Medications

Contraindicated for people with a personal or family history of medullary thyroid carcinoma (MTC) or Multiple Endocrine Neoplasia syndrome type 2 (MEN 2). Not recommended during pregnancy or breastfeeding. People with a history of pancreatitis or severe GI conditions require careful evaluation.

Always DiscloseYour complete medication list, all health conditions, and family history of thyroid cancer or MEN 2. Drug interactions exist and your physician needs the full picture.
3
Side Effects — What to Actually Expect

Most common: nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, constipation, and stomach discomfort. Most pronounced when starting and when increasing doses, typically diminishing significantly over time. Serious but less common: pancreatitis (rare), gallbladder disease (more common than previously recognized), and injection site reactions.

Managing Side EffectsEating smaller meals, avoiding high-fat foods at the start, and not eating close to injection time significantly reduce GI side effects. Many people who push through the first 4-8 weeks report side effects largely resolving.
4
The Muscle Loss Question

Roughly 25-40% of weight lost on GLP-1 medications is lean mass (muscle), not fat — similar to other calorie-deficit methods but worth taking seriously. This is one of the strongest arguments for combining these medications with resistance training and adequate protein intake. People who lose significant muscle mass may find weight regain easier when they eventually stop.

The PrescriptionResistance training 2-3x per week and a protein target of at least 0.7-1g per pound of target body weight. These aren't optional extras — they're central to long-term success.
5
What Happens When You Stop

Studies show the majority of people regain most lost weight within 1-2 years of stopping GLP-1 medications, because obesity is a chronic condition driven by biology — and the medication addresses it as long as it's taken. The "I'll take it to lose the weight and then stop" mental model is not well-supported by the evidence.

The Honest FramingThink of it like blood pressure medication — effective while taken, with return of the underlying condition when stopped. Long-term success requires either continuing or having made significant metabolic changes that can sustain results independently.
The Bottom Line

"These medications are tools. Powerful, validated, genuinely useful tools. What you build with them — the habits, the fitness, the relationship with food — is what lasts."

Part ThreeCost, Access & Getting Started
The Practical Questions

Cost, insurance, and
how to have the conversation

The gap between clinical evidence and real-world access is one of the most frustrating aspects of GLP-1 medications. Here's the current landscape.

1
The Cost Reality

Without insurance, brand-name GLP-1 medications cost $900-$1,400 per month in the U.S. Insurance coverage varies enormously: Medicare Part D currently does not cover weight-loss medications, many commercial plans don't cover them, and employer-sponsored plans are highly variable. Savings programs from manufacturers can significantly reduce costs for commercially insured patients.

Options to ExploreManufacturer savings cards (novocare.com and lillycares.com). Compounded semaglutide from licensed pharmacies (discuss with your doctor). Telehealth platforms that specialize in GLP-1 prescribing often have negotiated pricing.
2
Telehealth Prescribing Platforms

A number of telehealth platforms now specialize in GLP-1 prescribing — connecting patients with licensed physicians online, often at lower cost than traditional referrals. Calibrate, Ro Body, Found, and Sequence are among the established platforms. These vary in quality, monitoring intensity, and lifestyle support. Popcycle has reviewed the major platforms in our Weight Loss & Fitness section with PopScores.

What to Look ForPlatforms that include metabolic coaching, regular check-ins, and lab monitoring — not just prescription access. The medication works best with support.
3
How to Talk to Your Doctor

If your PCP hasn't brought up GLP-1 medications and you think you may be a candidate, bring it up directly. Come prepared: know your BMI, mention weight-related conditions, and ask specifically about semaglutide or tirzepatide. An endocrinologist or obesity medicine specialist may be a better starting point if your PCP is hesitant.

Part FourStraight Answers
Real Questions

What people actually
want to know

QCan I get Ozempic even though it's approved for diabetes?
Ozempic is frequently prescribed off-label for weight management. The active ingredient is identical to Wegovy — only the approved dose and indication differ. However, insurance coverage for off-label use is often denied, which is why your physician may recommend Wegovy specifically if weight management is the goal.
QHow long before I see results?
Most people notice reduced appetite within the first 1-4 weeks. Measurable weight loss typically begins in weeks 2-8. The titration period takes 16-20 weeks for most protocols. The most significant weight loss generally occurs between months 3-12. Patience with the titration process pays off — rushing to higher doses typically causes more side effects without proportionally better early results.
QIs this just for people who are very overweight?
FDA criteria start at BMI 27 with a weight-related condition, which covers a large portion of adults. These medications are increasingly being used for metabolic health reasons beyond scale weight, particularly in people with prediabetes, elevated cardiovascular risk, or fatty liver disease. Your physician can help evaluate whether the risk-benefit calculation makes sense for your specific situation.
QI've heard about hair loss. Is that real?
Yes, though it's not caused by the medication directly. Telogen effluvium — temporary hair shedding — is a well-documented response to rapid weight loss or significant calorie restriction, regardless of method. It typically appears 2-4 months after significant weight loss begins, is almost always temporary, and resolves as weight stabilizes. Adequate protein intake reduces the risk.
QAre these medications safe long-term?
GLP-1 medications have been used for Type 2 diabetes for 15+ years with a strong long-term safety record. The SELECT cardiovascular trial specifically demonstrated cardiovascular safety and benefit in people with overweight/obesity without diabetes. Long-term safety data specific to weight management populations is still accumulating, but the existing evidence is reassuring. Ongoing monitoring by your physician is the appropriate approach.

The right question isn't "Is Ozempic right for me?"
It's "What does the evidence say for my situation?"

GLP-1 medications represent a genuine advance in weight science. Whether they're right for you depends on your health profile, your goals, and a real conversation with your physician.
