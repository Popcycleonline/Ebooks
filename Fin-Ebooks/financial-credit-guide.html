Credit Monitoring & Score Tracking
Start Here, Always

You cannot improve what you cannot see.

Before spending money on credit repair, credit building, or any financial product in this category, the first step is always to get a clear, accurate picture of where your credit stands today. What are your scores across all three bureaus? What is dragging them down? What is your oldest account? Budgeting & Expense Tracking
The Honest Truth About Budgeting

Most people do not have an income problem. They have a visibility problem.

The research on financial behavior consistently shows that the simple act of seeing where money goes changes how people spend it. Not because shame motivates better decisions, but because awareness does. The tools in this section are different from each other in important ways — one is strict and methodological, one is more flexible and couple-friendly — and the best one for you is the one whose philosophy matches how your mind actually works.

Honest Answers

Questions we actually get

Q. How quickly can a credit score actually improve?
It depends on what is holding the score down. If the primary issue is a thin file with no negative marks, tools like Self or Kikoff can produce meaningful improvement in 3 to 6 months of consistent use. If there are negative items like late payments, collections, or high utilization, improvement timelines vary more significantly. Negative items generally age off credit reports after 7 years, though their impact diminishes well before that. There is no legitimate overnight fix.
Q. Is credit repair worth paying for?
Generally, no — if the goal is disputing inaccurate items on your credit report. You have the right to dispute inaccuracies directly with each bureau at no cost, and the process is the same whether you do it yourself or pay someone else to do it. What credit repair services can legitimately offer is expertise and time savings for complex situations with multiple disputes. If a service promises to remove accurate negative information, that is not something anyone can legitimately do, and it is a red flag.
Q. What credit score do I need to buy a house?
Conventional loans typically require a minimum score of 620, though the best interest rates are reserved for scores above 740. FHA loans can go as low as 580 with a 3.5% down payment. The score that matters for a mortgage is your FICO Score from the middle bureau of the three, which is why myFICO is particularly valuable for home purchase preparation — you need to see exactly what a lender will see.
Q. What hurts a credit score most?
In order of impact: payment history (35% of FICO score), credit utilization (30%), length of credit history (15%), new credit inquiries (10%), and credit mix (10%). A single missed payment can drop a score significantly, especially for someone with a previously clean history. High utilization — using more than 30% of available credit — is the most actionable lever most people can improve quickly, since it resets every month when balances update.
